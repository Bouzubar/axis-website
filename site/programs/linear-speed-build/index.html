Who it's for

Sprinters, jumpers and hurdlers who have built their base and are ready to train genuine top-end speed.

This is the block the whole year exists for, and the one most likely to end a season badly. Maximum velocity has to be trained at maximum velocity. No amount of squatting substitutes for it, and no drill replaces actually running fast. What the program does is build that exposure so the hamstring stays ahead of it: small weekly increases in fast metres, quality rules that end sets early, and eccentric hamstring work kept in even as everything else gets sharper. Strength stops climbing here and holds. Speed and elasticity take over.

Where it sits in the year: Block 2 of 4. Base, Build, Compete, Restore. Run it after Linear Speed · Base, in the specific preparation phase before your competitive season.

Your week

Two speed days and one strength-led day, always non-consecutive. Speed comes first in the session and first in the week.

Sun Mon Tue Wed Thu Fri Sat
Day 1 Sport Day 2 Sport Day 3 Rest Rest

Short on time? Do Day 1 and Day 2 only. Both keep their speed work, because the speed work is the block. The strength day is the one that goes.

Every session runs the same way:

  1. Warm-up, 8–10 min. The same sequence each time. Glutes, hips and shoulders switched on before anything fast happens.
  2. Speed and power, 12–15 min. Sprints, jumps and throws, with full recovery between sets. The set stops the moment quality drops.
  3. Strength, 20–25 min. The heavy compound lifts, with two to three minutes between sets.
  4. Tissue work, 8–10 min. Nordics, Copenhagens, calves and carries. Last, and never cut.

Speed and power come before fatigue, and heavy lifts before assistance. Tissue work goes last so it survives a rushed session instead of being cut from the middle.

How the 12 weeks progress

Three phases of four weeks each. Short accelerations first, then true top-speed running, then the sharpest and shortest work of the year. Strength is kept at low volume so the nervous system is free for speed. Fast running is prescribed in metres run above 90 per cent effort, and that weekly number is the most useful one in the block.

Run the four blocks in order and they make a full training year.

RPE is effort rated out of 10. Reps in reserve is how many more good reps you could have done, so "about 2 reps in reserve" means about 2 reps left in the tank.

Deload weeks: weeks 6 and 12 cut the fast metres roughly in half and halve the sets. Retest fresh, never tired.

The rules that make it work

  • Count your fast metres. Write down every rep above 90 per cent effort and keep the weekly total inside the phase target. Spikes in fast running are what tear hamstrings, not fast running itself.
  • The set ends when the times end. A tenth of a second slower is the signal. Stop the set and take the rest. A speed session must not drift into conditioning because the plan said six reps.
  • Never two speed days back to back. Top-speed work needs 48 hours. Two fast days in a row is the most reliable way to turn a good block into a torn hamstring.
  • Strength holds, speed climbs. Loads sit at RPE 7–8 with three to four sets. You cannot chase gym records and sprint records in the same twelve weeks, and this block picks speed.
  • Nordics survive everything. Even in the sharpening phase, even in deload weeks. If the hamstring test falls while the fly time improves, the metres come down straight away.
  • Count your landings, not just your sets. Every jump, hop, bound and drop is a landing your tendons pay for, warm-up hops included. Throws and sprints are not landings. Keep the week under about 150 landings in this block, with sport practice counted on top.
Terms
  • Block: 12 weeks. This program. One block develops one set of qualities to a set standard.
  • Phase (mesocycle): 4 weeks. Each one targets one quality. Four weeks is a choice, not a rule. The length follows the goal.
  • Training week (microcycle): where effort climbs, sets are added and the deload lands.
  • Training year (macrocycle): four blocks in order (Base, Build, Compete, Restore), about 48 weeks.

How you'll measure progress

Weeks 6 and 12 are retest weeks, done fresh at the end of each deload. Every week also starts with a 90-second check: three standing broad jumps at the start of Day 1, keeping the best. More than 10 per cent below your own rolling average means that day is reduced. The power work comes out and each lift loses one set, whatever the page says.

What good progress looks like

  • Weeks 1–4: accelerations feel powerful and the sled work pays off quickly.
  • Weeks 5–8: top speed arrives and the fly time drops. This is also when hamstrings complain if the metres climbed too fast.
  • Weeks 9–12: everything shortens and sharpens, and the fastest running of the year happens on the least tired legs.

A block finished healthy with a slightly slower time beats a personal best in week 8 and a torn hamstring in week 9.

Respect the week

Count everything: matches, sport training, gym and sleep. Sudden jumps in weekly workload are the most reliable predictor of injury in amateur sport. In a heavy sport week, drop a gym session or cut the power work, but never cut both the sport and the recovery. Protect 7–9 hours of sleep. It is the most powerful performance tool available, and the cheapest.

The science

Every program in the library is built on published research, and how strong that research is gets stated, not implied. Where the evidence is limited, the page says so.

Programs that include the Nordic hamstring exercise roughly halve hamstring injuries. van Dyk, Behan & Whiteley, Br J Sports Med 2019. Meta-analysis, 15 studies, 8,459 athletes. PMID 30808663 Risk ratio 0.49. The effect shrinks but survives when the eight high-risk-of-bias studies are removed (0.55). It measures programs that include the exercise, not the exercise alone.
