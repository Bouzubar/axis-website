Why programs run in blocks, with lighter weeks planned in

Planned changes in training beat doing the same thing every week, at least for strength.

The short answer

Planned, changing training (periodization) gives slightly more strength than doing the same thing every week. Lighter weeks that cut sets, rather than stopping completely, don't seem to cost progress.

Every AXIS program is a block of weeks with a plan: what you build first, what comes next, and when you ease off. This is called periodization. Here is why it is worth the effort.

Planned beats random, for strength

A 2022 analysis compared periodized programs with non-periodized ones, with the total amount of training kept equal. Periodized training gave more strength (a small effect). Muscle growth was the same either way. Programs that changed the load day to day (undulating) beat ones that changed it slowly (linear) for strength, but only in people who already trained.

In plain words: for muscle, the total work matters most. For strength, how you arrange that work adds a little extra.

Lighter weeks: reduce, don't stop

A lighter week (a deload) is a planned easier week to let fatigue clear before the next push. The research is young, but two trials point the same way:

  • In a 2024 trial, trained people who took a full week off midway through a 9-week program grew the same amount of muscle, but gained less strength than those who kept training.
  • In a 2026 trial, untrained men who cut back to 2 sets once a week in weeks 4 and 8 gained the same muscle and strength endurance as when they trained straight through.

A 2024 survey of 246 strength and physique athletes found most of them plan a lighter week roughly every 5 to 6 weeks, cutting sets and effort but keeping their usual exercises.

How AXIS uses this

Lighter weeks reduce the work, they don't remove it. In Foundation Strength, weeks 6 and 12 have half the usual sets at the same weights, so fatigue clears and you retest fresh. The sports programs run in four blocks (Base, Build, Compete, Restore) so your hardest training lands before your season, not during it.

The limits

Deload research is still small and short. Strength may dip slightly after a full break, so AXIS keeps you training through lighter weeks.
