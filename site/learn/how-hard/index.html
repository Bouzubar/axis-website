How hard should a set feel? Effort 7/10 explained

You don't need to train to failure to get stronger. You do need to get close for muscle.

The short answer

An effort of 7/10 means you could have done about 3 more clean reps. Strength gains look similar across a wide range of effort, while muscle growth improves as sets end closer to failure.

Every AXIS program tells you how hard each set should feel, as a number out of 10. It is the simplest way to size a workout to you, whatever weight you lift.

The scale

Think about how many more clean reps you could have done when the set ended.

  • 10/10: none. You could not do another rep.
  • 9/10: 1 more.
  • 8/10: 2 more.
  • 7/10: about 3 more.
  • 6/10: about 4 more. Hard work, but smooth.

Clean means the same technique as your first rep. If your form breaks down, the set is over, whatever the number.

What the research says

A 2024 analysis looked at how close to failure people trained across many studies. Two different patterns appeared:

  • Strength: gains were similar across a wide range of effort. Training to failure was not needed to get stronger.
  • Muscle: growth improved as sets ended closer to failure.

The authors called the analysis exploratory, because most studies did not record effort directly and it had to be estimated. The direction is still useful.

How AXIS uses this

Effort climbs across a block. In Foundation Strength it starts around 6/10 while you learn the movements and rises to about 8/10 in the strength phase. Heavy, technical lifts stay a little further from failure. Smaller muscle-building exercises can be taken closer, where the extra growth is worth it and the injury risk is low.

If you are new, expect to underestimate how many reps you have left. That is normal, and it gets more accurate within a few weeks.
