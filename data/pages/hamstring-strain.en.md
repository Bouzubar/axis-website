## Who this is for

Athletes recovering from a hamstring muscle strain who have been diagnosed by a physiotherapist or doctor, are cleared to run, and want to get back to full speed without tearing it again.

Hamstring strains come back often, usually because the muscle returned to sprinting before it was strong in long positions. This program builds that strength first, with daily lengthening work, then brings the speed back in tested steps.

**How long it takes:** strains that happened while sprinting fit this 6-week plan. Strains that happened in a stretch (kicking, splits, slide tackles) usually take longer, so expect to repeat weeks.

**Not for:** a tear of the tendon at the sitting bone (a pop high under the buttock with big bruising), an injury that needed surgery, or pain with numbness or tingling down the leg. These need a doctor first.

## Your week

Four training days, plus a short lengthening routine every day, rest days included. Running comes first so the hamstring is fresh for sprints.

| Sun | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 1** Run + daily routine | **Day 2** Gym + daily routine | Daily routine | **Day 3** Run + daily routine | **Day 4** Gym + daily routine | Daily routine | Daily routine |

**How it runs:**
1. **Entry Check first.** If any line is not true, you don't start.
2. **Test before Week 1.** Range, strength and a sprint check.
3. **Daily lengthening, 10 minutes.** Three exercises that load the hamstring in long positions.
4. **Pass each gate to move on.** If you don't pass, you repeat the last week and retest.

## How the 6 weeks progress

| | Weeks 1–2 | Weeks 3–4 | Weeks 5–6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase** | Reload | Strength and Running | Maximum Speed and Sport |
| **Focus** | Holds and controlled strength in longer positions, running up to 70% | Nordics start, heavier hinges, running up to 90% | Heavy and fast hamstring work, full-speed sprints, sport drills and team training |
| **Gate at the end** | Range within 15°, sprint at 70% pain-free | Range within 10°, sprint at 90% pain-free | Pain-free 100% sprint, one full team training |

### The rules that make it work
- **Long before fast.** The hamstring gets strong in lengthened positions before it is asked to sprint.
- **Speed goes up in steps.** Every sprint is a set percentage of flat out, and each step has to be pain-free before the next.
- **Pain sets the pace.** A pain table tells you whether to carry on, hold or step back.

## How you'll measure progress

At Week 0, Week 2, Week 4 and Week 6 you test:
- **Range:** an active straight-leg raise, measured with a phone app.
- **Strength:** single-leg bridge reps to fatigue, each leg.
- **Confidence:** a fast kick test, from Week 4 only.
- **Speed:** the fastest pain-free 30 m sprint, as a percentage of flat out.

**Exit standard at Week 6:** range within 10° of the other side, bridge reps at 90% or more of the other leg and at least 20, no doubt on the fast kick, a pain-free 100% sprint, and one full team training with no pain before you play a match.

## Before you buy: you must pass this entry check

All of these must be true before you start:
- Diagnosed with a hamstring muscle strain by a physiotherapist or doctor, and discharged or cleared to run.
- Walking and easy jogging without pain.
- Straight-leg raise within 20° of the other side, without sharp pain.
- A pain-free single-leg glute bridge hold for 30 seconds on the injured side.

Stop and contact your physiotherapist or doctor if you feel sudden sharp pain or a pop, see new bruising on the back of the thigh, feel numbness or tingling down the leg, or the pain gets worse from one week to the next.

This program does not replace your physiotherapist or doctor. When you buy, you confirm that you have been diagnosed with a hamstring muscle strain and cleared to run.
