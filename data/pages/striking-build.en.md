## Who it's for

Strikers who have a strength base and want to turn it into speed before camp. Striking Base built the base. Build is where the numbers get serious: this is the heaviest lifting of your year, and it belongs here because no fight is booked.

Then it gets converted. Heavy strength on its own does not make a faster punch. Strength expressed quickly does, and that conversion is what the second half of the block is for. Throws get faster, jumps get sharper, and loads move with intent instead of grinding. The shoulder, calf and neck work stays exactly where it was, every session, because camp is coming and those three tissues are the ones camp wears down.

**Where it sits in the year:** Block 2 of 4. Base, Build, Compete, Restore, run in order. Run it before camp opens, when sparring is moderate and nothing is booked.

## Your week

Three gym days around your sport. The loads climb while the sessions get shorter, and phase 3 is the shortest heavy training of the year. Never heavy legs within six hours of hard sparring. Tired legs mean a head that stops moving, and that is how people get hurt in the gym.

**Off-season, 3 gym days**

| Sun | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Sport | **Day 2** | Sport | **Day 3** | Rest | Rest |

**In-season, 2 gym days**

| Sun | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Sport | **Day 2** | Rest | Sport | Match | Rest |

**Every session runs the same way:**
1. **Warm-up, 8–10 min.** The same sequence each time, plus wrists and neck. Never skipped, never rushed.
2. **Elastic and power, 12–15 min.** Med-ball throws, bounds and footwork, fresh and sharp, with full recovery between sets.
3. **Strength, 20–25 min.** The heavy compound lifts with long rests. This is force, not another round.
4. **Shoulders, neck and calves, 10 min.** Last, and never cut.

Nothing in this block is trained to exhaustion. Your sport already does that better than any gym session could.

## How the 12 weeks progress

Three phases of four weeks each. Maximal strength and rate of force development are related but not the same, and a striker needs the second. The reliable way to get it is a strength phase followed by a conversion phase: build the force first, then train the nervous system to express it in the fraction of a second a punch or kick takes. Loads peak in the first half of the block, and bar speed becomes the target in the second. The tissue work follows a different logic. Shoulder endurance, calf capacity and neck strength respond to steady, unglamorous volume, so they close every session in every phase.

| | Weeks 1–4 | Weeks 5–8 | Weeks 9–12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase** | Maximal strength | Explosive strength | Elastic and speed |
| **Goal** | Sets of four to five at real load. Power work stays sharp but low in volume. | Heavy loads moved fast, every rep driven with intent. Power volume doubles. | Reactive strength and speed before camp. Speed leads, a little heavy work holds the strength. |
| **Working sets per week** | 62 | 67 | 62 |
| **Average effort** | RPE 7.9, about 2 reps in reserve | RPE 7.9, about 2 reps in reserve | RPE 7.2, about 3 reps in reserve |
| **Landings per week** | 147 | 86 | 120 |
| **Throws per week** | 50 | 56 | 52 |

RPE is a 1–10 rating of how hard a set feels. Reps in reserve is how many more good reps you could have done, so about 2 means 2 reps left in the tank.

**Deload weeks:** weeks 6 and 12 halve the sets and cut the jumps and throws to half volume. You retest fresh.

### The rules that make it work
- **This is the heaviest block you will run.** Sets of four to five at real load, now, because nothing is booked. You will not get a better window.
- **Then convert it, or it stays in the gym.** Heavy strength does not automatically become a faster shot. The second half trains speed of force with throws, jumps and fast intent on the bar.
- **Power always goes first.** Throws, jumps and hops open the session. Done tired they are impact without speed.
- **The tissue work never changes.** Shoulders, calves and neck in every session, every phase. Camp will take everything from those three, so arrive with a surplus.
- **Nothing heavy before hard sparring.** Six hours minimum, or a different day.
- **Use a jump number as a fatigue test.** Three standing broad jumps at the start of Day 1 each week, best one kept. More than 10% below your own rolling average means reduce that day: drop the power work and one set from each lift.
- **Count landings, not just sets.** Every jump, hop, bound and drop is a landing your tendons pay for, warm-up hops included. Med-ball throws, sprints and split steps are not landings. Keep the week under about 150 landings in Build, with sport practice on top.

### Terms (expandable)
- **Training year (macrocycle):** four blocks in order (Base, Build, Compete, Restore), about 48 weeks.
- **Block:** 12 weeks. This program. One block develops one set of qualities to a set standard.
- **Phase (mesocycle):** 4 weeks. Each one targets one quality. Four weeks is a choice, not a rule. The length follows the goal.
- **Training week (microcycle):** where effort climbs, sets are added and the deload lands.

## How you'll measure progress

The weekly broad jump tracks fatigue, and you retest fresh in the deload weeks, 6 and 12. Body weight should be stable and known, so that when camp opens you are not starting from a guess.

**What good progress looks like**
- **Weeks 1–4:** the bar gets genuinely heavy, and you will feel it in the first sparring session of each week. Keep the lifting off those days.
- **Weeks 5–8:** strength numbers peak, and this is where most athletes see throw distance jump.
- **Weeks 9–12:** sessions get short and sharp, and the speed shows up on the pads before it shows up in the gym.

## Respect the week

Count everything: fights, sport training, gym and sleep. Sudden jumps in weekly workload are the most reliable predictor of injury in amateur sport. In a heavy sport week, drop a gym session or cut the power work, never both the sport and the recovery. Protect 7–9 hours of sleep. It is the most powerful performance tool you have, and the cheapest.

## The science

Every program in the library is built on published research, and how strong that research is gets stated, not implied. Where the evidence is limited, the page says so.

| Finding | Source | What it does not show |
|---|---|---|
| Strength training cuts sports injuries to under a third of the control rate. Stretching does not reduce them. | Lauersen, Bertelsen & Andersen, *British Journal of Sports Medicine* 2014. | Every sensible prescription beats not training. Heavier loads rank highest for strength.
