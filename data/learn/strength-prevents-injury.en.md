Most athletes warm up with stretching and call it injury prevention. The research points somewhere else.

## Strength beats stretching

A 2014 analysis pooled 25 randomised trials with 26,610 athletes. Strength training cut sports injuries to **under a third** of the rate in people who did not do it. Stretching made **no difference**. Balance training and mixed programs sat in between. Overuse injuries were almost halved.

## One exercise can matter

Some injuries respond to a single, targeted exercise:

- **Hamstrings.** Programs that include the Nordic hamstring curl roughly **halve** hamstring injuries, across 15 studies and 8,459 athletes.
- **Groin.** In a trial with 35 football teams, one adductor exercise (the Copenhagen adduction), done three times a week in pre-season and once a week in season, lowered the odds of groin problems by **41%**.

## How AXIS uses this

Every sports program builds these in where the sport needs them, for example Nordic curls for sprinting and field sports, and adductor work for football and other change-of-direction sports. They stay in the program through the season at a lower dose, because the protection fades if you stop.

## The limits

The trials vary a lot, and doses were not standardised. The Nordic finding is about programs that include it, not the exercise alone. The groin trial was in semi-professional men's football only. Stretching still has a place for range of motion. It just isn't injury prevention on its own.
