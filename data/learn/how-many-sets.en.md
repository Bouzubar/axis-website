"How many sets?" is one of the most studied questions in strength training, so we can answer it with more confidence than most.

## First: almost anything works

A 2023 analysis pooled 178 strength studies and 119 muscle-growth studies. Every training prescription they looked at beat doing nothing, for both strength and muscle. Heavier loads ranked highest for strength, and multiple sets ranked highest for muscle size. The gaps between prescriptions were small.

So if you are not training yet, the best number of sets is the one you will actually do.

## More sets, more growth, with diminishing returns

Count sets per muscle per week. A 2017 analysis found a graded effect: each extra weekly set added about 0.37% more muscle growth.

The largest analysis so far, from 2025, covered 67 studies and 2,058 people. More weekly sets meant more muscle and more strength, but the extra benefit shrank as sets went up, and it shrank faster for strength than for muscle. Training a muscle more often in the week helped strength. For muscle size, how often mattered much less than how much.

## How AXIS uses this

- Every working exercise gets at least 3 sets. Warm-ups, mobility and lighter weeks are the exceptions.
- Weekly sets build gradually across a block instead of starting high.
- Physique programs give the most sets to the muscles they are built around, for example the glutes in the glute-emphasis programs.

## The limits

Most studies last 8 to 12 weeks and use young, healthy adults, and few go beyond about 10 or more sets per muscle per week. More is not always better: the right number is the most you can recover from and keep progressing on.
